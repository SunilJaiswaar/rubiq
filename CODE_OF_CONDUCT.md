# Code of Conduct

## The point

This project exists to help people learn, including people who are at the very beginning
and feel stupid about it. Almost everything in this document follows from that.

## Expected

- **Assume the question is reasonable.** If someone asks something you find obvious, they
  are not wasting your time — they are telling you where an explanation failed. That is
  useful information.
- **Review content and code, not people.** "This explanation assumes the reader already
  knows what a closure is" is useful. "Did you even read the guide?" is not.
- **Be specific.** "This is wrong" helps nobody. "This says hash lookup is O(1) worst case;
  it is O(1) average and O(n) worst case" fixes a lesson.
- **Accept that you might be wrong,** and say so plainly when you are. A maintainer who
  cannot do this makes a project nobody wants to contribute to.

## Not accepted

- **Condescension toward beginners.** This is the specific behaviour most directly opposed
  to the project's purpose, and it is treated as seriously as any other violation. "RTFM",
  "this is basic", and sighing in prose at someone's confusion are all out.
- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments on the basis of any personal characteristic.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or deliberate derailing.

## Scope

Applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space where you
are representing the project.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainers via
[GitHub's private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/SunilJaiswaar/rubiq/security/advisories/new)
or by opening an issue if the matter is public and minor. Reports are handled
confidentially.

Maintainers will respond with whatever is proportionate — usually a private word, which
resolves most things. Repeated or severe violations lead to a temporary or permanent ban
from the project's spaces.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this in good faith may themselves face
consequences. Being a maintainer is not an exemption.
